# Approval and continuity

Keep a project ledger: mode, style/version, aspect, duration, provider/route, source roles, approved images, anatomy/variant, expression, props and ownership, tabletop contact points, stack count, geography, head-end orientation, screen direction, light/weather, action state, shot rhythm, audio, prompt limit, and repairs.

Explicit user direction and approved project state govern scene details; underlying identity remains anchored to source and selected sheet. Do not infer user approval from a successful generation or internal visual review.

Preserve one head/four supports per Mensa. Every stack member remains separate and retains its own top, feet, and head. Prevent objects or feet fusing into adjacent bodies. Record front/head end and rear end before moving cameras. Keep feet and props attached to their owners when occluded.

Progress actions instead of resetting: carry contact remains; moved objects stay moved; landed feet remain planted; a cracked surface stays cracked; completed power-up aura persists when requested. Avoid assigning palm ownership to a character with no hands.

For repair use:

```text
LOCK: [passing identity, pose, environment, style, composition, props and action]
CHANGE ONLY: [smallest failed layer]
DO NOT CHANGE: [all other approved state]
```

Repair identity/anatomy first, prop count/contact next, construction and project state next, then rendering, light, and decoration. Regenerate only a failed shot where practical. Simplify overlapping movement before adding effects. For transformation, define PRE-STATE / CHANGE ONLY / POST-STATE and bridge fixed states through a cut or occlusion; never morph the table into an upright fighter.

Episode board 2 begins at board 1's approved endpoint, and so on. Do not generate all boards in advance unless explicitly requested.
