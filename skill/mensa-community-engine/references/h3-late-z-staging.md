## Choose readable concepts

For an open brief, favor one main character, one location, one decisive action,
and an aftermath that visibly proves the action. Use a readable opening hook,
physical escalation, and a payoff with time to register. Vary the environment
and kind of consequence across a set rather than repeating a desert explosion.

| Scene family | Useful construction | Observed constraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Environmental power-up | Stable pose; escalating floor, rain, water, or debris response | Avoid long unchanged ready poses and aura obscuring identity |
| Concealment and reveal | Rubble/smoke hides the Mensa; pressure builds; the Mensa emerges unharmed | Preserve the Mensa's location and show the obstruction actually clearing |
| Single strike, large consequence | One contact; fracture or distant impact proves its force | Do not leave the opponent beside the Mensa after the hit |
| Vertical ascent or lift | One vertical path; fixed platform, waterline, or mountain base | Hide the full scale until the intended reveal |
| Short melee | Dodge, strike, and reaction in separate readable beats | Extended exchanges and overlapping limbs are less reliable |
| Continuous energy redirection | Preserve contact, incoming path, reversal, and outgoing path | Beam bending repeatedly lost geometry or causal continuity |

Do not replace an explicit fight, beam, location, or complex premise with a
preferred family. Simplify its staging first. Offer a compact sphere instead
of a beam only when the user permits a premise change.

## Build states, not adjective stacks

For each hard action, specify visible before-state -> transition -> after-state.
Examples: four feet brace under pressure -> tabletop rises slightly as supports straighten -> floor buckles;
punch lands -> target briefly holds -> crack spreads -> structure separates.
Give each principal shot one job: threat, decision, contact, consequence, or
reveal. Around four or five principal shots with brief inserts is a useful
15-second starting point, not a fixed quota.

- Make anticipation progress through a concrete cue rather than repeating a
  pose: knees buckle, front foot presses into stone, or a fissure widens.
- Compress contact and travel. Put a brief strike and its immediate consequence
  within a beat; avoid assigning a multi-second shot solely to a fast punch.
- After contact, cut to the opponent already displaced, then a distant impact.
  Retain the hit's direction and established destination across cuts.
- Use flashes or occlusion to bridge difficult transitions only when the next
  image makes the consequence obvious. A flash cannot explain an ambiguous
  beam reversal or replace visible body-object contact when that is essential.
- For a last-instant dodge, establish absence before impact: the Mensa leaves
  the path, the fist hits visibly empty rock, then reveal the new position. Use an
  afterimage only when it contributes to the requested premise.
- Reserve the largest information change for the payoff. A close-up of cracks
  can tease a mountain lift without showing the mountain already airborne.
- Finish with physical evidence: separated pillar, ejected door, elevated
  mountain, cleared rubble, or opponent's distant impact. Preserve the changed
  world in the aftermath rather than resetting it.

Timecodes are guidance, not exact editing guarantees. Describe fast motion in
terms of departure, contact, displacement, and cuts; `no slow motion` alone was
insufficient. If exact delivered duration or ratio matters, inspect the media
metadata and use a suitable export without claiming the model hit exact specs.

## Projectile construction without invented hands

Mensa has no palms. Do not copy palm formation, catching, gauntlets, or throwing
mechanics from humanoid examples. If the user requests an energy orb, resolve an
anatomically compatible origin, such as floating above the tabletop, before
changing their intended action. A free-floating orb is a project-specific
creative effect, not a canonical ability.

Separate formation, internal spin, and straight fast travel. Keep diameter stable
until impact. Show one detachment and an established target; empty air behind
the detached sphere prevents beam-like output. Compress travel and cut to impact
instead of a slow tracking shot. Retain requested effect colors and payoff.

A catch-and-return needs a supported contact mechanism and continuous ownership;
do not hide unexplained capture or reversal inside a flash. If the mechanism is
unclear, ask a short staging question rather than inventing hands.

## Reference and prompt economy

Keep the approved Late-Z sheet as Image 1 and the sole default R2V reference.
The Yuki session supported recognizable identity with its own sheet; it did not
validate Mensa outcomes or establish that extra references are never useful.
Add narrow authorities only under the existing route rules. Do not add raw broadcast frames to cure action staging.
Use one compact identity/rendering lock, chronological action blocks, an audio
clause, and a few failure-specific negatives. Prefer rewriting an ambiguous
action to accumulating more contradictory exclusions.

## Audio and review

For Late-Z H3 clips, default to sound effects and environment only, with
`NO MUSIC`, unless the user requests music. Distinguish this from `NO AUDIO`.
Carry the user's choice across reruns and repairs. Specify no score, musical
bed, or melodic drone when needed; keep electrical effects irregular. Prompt
wording or a provider's expanded prompt is not proof of the returned audio.

For requested generation, review available visuals for identity, cause/effect,
contact, direction, release speed, and payoff. Label what was actually checked:
sampled frames establish sampled appearance/states, not all motion or audio.
Do not claim music-free verification without a supported audio check, and do
not launch additional external analysis when it is outside the authorized scope
or the user declines it. A prompt-only no-music request needs no extra audit.

Repair the smallest failed layer while preserving the requested premise. If a
paid rerun is already authorized, make at most one targeted repair per failed
clip before returning the result and any remaining limitation; do not silently
turn a batch into repeated billable experiments. If a retry is outside scope,
deliver completed work and describe the concrete proposed repair.

| Failure | Targeted repair |
| --- | --- |
| Beam bends into the ground or loses contact | Re-establish contact and direction; simplify the reversal shots; discuss a sphere alternative only if permitted |
| Orb becomes a beam or drifts slowly | Isolated ball, no connecting trail, brief release-to-contact transition |
| Melee contact lingers | Instant hit insert, then opponent already traveling toward the established impact site |
| Reveal is spent early | Replace the early wide with a pressure/crack detail; show the full changed state only at payoff |
| Aura obscures the Mensa or washes out skin | Restore warm-brown cel planes and recognizable face; move effects behind the silhouette and reduce foreground arcs |
| Power-up stalls | Add one progressing physical cue; preserve anatomy and the stable pose |
